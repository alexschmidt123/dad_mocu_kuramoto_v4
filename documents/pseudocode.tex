\textbf{Short overview.}
sBOED for swing dynamics: latent $\theta=(H,K)$; downstream decision quantity $\gamma^\star(\theta)$ (minimum gain for frequency security under fixed $d_{\mathrm{ref}}$). At each step: choose $\xi_t$, observe $y_t$, update $p_t(\theta)$; choose designs to reduce $\MOCU(p_t)$ (posterior decision uncertainty in $\gamma^\star(\theta)$).

\medskip
\noindent
\textbf{Notation.}
$\theta=(H,K)$ latent; $\xi_t$ design; $y_t$ observation; $p_t(\theta)$ posterior; $\gamma$ control variable; $\gamma^\star(\theta)$ minimum safe gain; $\MOCU(p_t)$ decision uncertainty in $\gamma^\star(\theta)$. Chain: $\xi_t\to y_t\to p_t(\theta)\to$ uncertainty in $\gamma^\star(\theta)\to\MOCU(p_t)$.

\section{Framework: Goals, Model, and Sequential Bayesian Design}
\label{sec:setup}

Pipeline: swing dynamics (latent $\theta=(H,K)$) $\to$ probing ($\xi_t$, $y_t$) $\to$ posterior $p_t(\theta)$ $\to$ evaluation under $d_{\mathrm{ref}}$ $\to$ $\gamma^\star(\theta)$ $\to$ $\MOCU(p_t)$. $\gamma$ = downstream control. $M=2HS_n/\omega_s$; DAD: $\Nouter$ outer, $\Ninner$ support (Foster et al., 2021).

% -----------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Stability condition (grid codes; textbook)}
\label{sec:definitions}

\textbf{Source: textbook / grid codes (e.g.\ Kundur; ENTSO-E, NERC).} Security requires that over a contingency horizon frequency $f(t)$ and ROCOF $\dot f(t)$ stay within limits:
\[
\forall t\in[0,T_c],\quad |\dot f(t)|\le r_{\max}\ \ \text{and}\ \ f(t)\ge f_{\min}.
\]
These limits are used in the definition of the model-specific minimum safe gain $\gamma^\star(\theta)$ (Section~\ref{sec:decision}).

% -----------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Dynamics model (from probing paper)}
\label{sec:model}

\textbf{Source: Peng et al.\ (2024), Eq.\ (1)--(3).} The probing paper models the system with inertia constant $H$ (MWs/MVA), aggregate primary-frequency-response droop constant $K$ (MW/Hz), and $S_n$ = rated apparent power (base power, e.g.\ MVA). The swing equation (Eq.\ (1) of the paper) is
\[
\frac{2HS_n}{\omega_s}\,\frac{d\omega(t)}{dt}+D\,\omega(t)=P_m(t)-P_e(t),
\]
with mechanical power $P_m$ and electrical power $P_e$.

\paragraph{Aggregate droop in the plant model (Peng et al.).}
Peng et al.\ model \textbf{aggregate droop} (they refer to $K$ as an aggregate primary-frequency-response droop constant in MW/Hz). Let $\Delta f(t)=[\omega(t)-\omega(0)]/(2\pi)$ (frequency deviation in Hz). Then
\[
\Delta P_m(t)\;=\;-K\,\Delta f(t),
\qquad\text{equivalently}\quad
P_m(t)\;=\;P_m(0)-\frac{K}{2\pi}\big[\omega(t)-\omega(0)\big].
\]
With this droop model, writing $\Delta P_e(t)=P_e(t)-P_e(0)$, $\Delta\omega(t)=\omega(t)-\omega(0)$, and assuming $P_m(0)=P_e(0)+D\omega(0)$ in steady state gives the \textbf{reduced swing dynamics} (Eq.\ (3) of the paper):
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:swing3}
\boxed{\;\frac{2HS_n}{\omega_s}\,\frac{d\omega(t)}{dt}=-\Delta P_e(t)-\left(\frac{K}{2\pi}+D\right)\Delta\omega(t).\;}
\end{equation}
This is the \textbf{closed-loop short-time frequency-response model} used for evaluation. The probing paper uses it for inertia and droop estimation from $\omega$ and $P_e$ measurements after a disturbance; our framework uses the same model as the physical basis for sequential design. In \textbf{our} sBOED formulation, $K$ is \textbf{latent} (part of $\theta$): it is a \textbf{coefficient in the ODE}, not a control input we set. Any supplementary gain $\gamma$ introduced in Section~\ref{sec:decision} is modeled as an additional feedback input, not a change to $K$.

\paragraph{Terminology: avoid ``primary control'' for $K$ or $\xi$.}
We reserve \textbf{supplementary control} for the actuation $u_{\mathrm{ctrl}}(\cdot;\gamma)$ and gain $\gamma\ge 0$ used in the \textbf{evaluation} model for frequency security (Section~\ref{sec:decision}). The droop coefficient $K$ is \textbf{not} ``primary control'' in the sense of a knob that is known or chosen precisely after experiments---it remains uncertain. The probe design $\xi$ is \textbf{design of experiments} for learning, not operational plant control. For comparison, uncertain Kuramoto OED (Chen et al., 2023; Hong et al., 2021) introduces a \textbf{control oscillator} with coupling $a_{N+1}$; the minimum nonnegative $a_{N+1}$ needed for synchronization defines an operational cost (Chen et al.\ denote this map by $\xi(\mathbf{a})$---not to be confused with our probe design $\xi=(b,A,T_p)$). \textbf{That} cost role parallels \textbf{$\gamma$ and $\gamma^\star(\theta)$} here, not latent~$K$.

\paragraph{Relationship between $M$ and $H$.}
The coefficient of $d\omega/dt$ is often denoted $M$: $M=2HS_n/\omega_s$, so $H=M\omega_s/(2S_n)$. Then $M\,\frac{d\omega(t)}{dt}=-\Delta P_e(t)-\left(\frac{K}{2\pi}+D\right)\Delta\omega(t)$. The latent parameter is $\theta=(H,K)$ (or $(M,K)$ via the $M$--$H$ relation).

The paper integrates the reduced dynamics to obtain Eq.\ (4), linking $H$, $K$ to $\omega(t)$ and $P_e(t)$ for regression-based estimation. Our sBOED layer adds prior, likelihood, and posterior over $\theta$.

\paragraph{Probing signal (from paper).}
\textbf{Source: Peng et al.\ (2024).} A probing signal (e.g.\ Hann window, 2\,s duration) is injected by an IBR to generate informative transients in $\omega$ and $P_e$. The \textbf{design variable} $\xi_t$ is the probing experiment setting at stage $t$: e.g.\ bus, amplitude, and probing duration. We take $\xi=(b,A,T_p)$ (bus, amplitude, duration). The \textbf{probing injection} is $u^{\mathrm{probe}}(\cdot;\xi)$; it drives $\Delta P_e(t)$ and the transients used for estimation.

\paragraph{Multi-bus network (our extension).}
For an IEEE-style network we use a multi-bus swing model parametrized by $(H,K)$ so that the forward simulation produces $\omega(t)$ and $P_e(t)$. \textbf{Added by our framework:} network topology $\mathcal{G}$, susceptance $\bm{B}$, and a fixed evaluation disturbance $d_{\mathrm{ref}}$ (contingency) for defining the decision quantity $\gamma^\star(\theta)$ below.

% -----------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Latent parameters (from probing paper)}
\label{sec:latent}

\textbf{Source: Peng et al.\ (2024).} The latent uncertain parameter is $\theta=(H,K)$, where $H$ is the inertia constant and $K$ is the aggregate primary-frequency-response droop constant. We write $\theta=(H,K)\in\Theta\subseteq\mathbb{R}_+^2$, or equivalently $\theta=(M,K)$ with $M=2HS_n/\omega_s$. The paper estimates $(H,K)$ from $\omega(t)$ and $P_e(t)$ via the integrated relation (Eq.\ (4)); it does \emph{not} provide a prior or posterior. Our framework uses a discrete grid $\{\theta_{\ninner}\}_{\ninner=1}^{\Ninner}$ with weights $\{p_t^{\ninner}\}$; the \textbf{discrete belief} representation is \textbf{added by our framework}.

% -----------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Prior (our sBOED extension)}
\label{sec:prior}

\textbf{Added by our framework; not from the probing paper.} The prior $p_0(\theta)$ over $\Theta$ is chosen by us (e.g.\ uniform over a bounded box in $(H,K)$, or over a discrete set $\{\theta_{\ninner}\}_{\ninner=1}^{\Ninner}$ with weights $\{p_0^{\ninner}\}$, $\sum p_0^{\ninner}=1$). The paper does not use a prior; it uses regression only.

% -----------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Design and design space (order matters)}
\label{sec:design}

\paragraph{Design space.}
\textbf{From paper:} probing signal settings (the paper uses a Hann signal, 2\,s). \textbf{Our parameterization:} $\Xi$ = set of single-step probe designs $\xi=(b,A,T_p)$ (bus, amplitude, duration). At step $t$ we choose $\xi_t\in\Xi$. A fixed \textbf{evaluation disturbance} $d_{\mathrm{ref}}$ (contingency) and fixed horizon $[0,T_c]$ are used when defining the decision quantity $\gamma^\star(\theta)$ (Section~\ref{sec:decision}).

\paragraph{Order matters.}
A \emph{design sequence} is an ordered list $\xi_{1:T}=(\xi_1,\dots,\xi_T)$. Different sequences yield different observations and posteriors, hence different terminal values of $\MOCU(p_T)$. A myopic rule that at each step $t$ chooses $\xi_t$ to minimize $\mathbb{E}_{y_t\mid p_{t-1},\xi_t}[\MOCU(p_t)]$ is tractable but generally suboptimal for terminal $\MOCU(p_T)$.

% -----------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Observation: paper vs.\ our simplification}
\label{sec:observation}

\textbf{From the probing paper:} The observation $y_t$ in the paper is the measured data (e.g.\ $\omega(t)$ and $P_e(t)$) from the transient after applying $\xi_t$; the paper uses these for regression-based estimation of $H$ and $K$.

\textbf{Our simplification for sBOED (added by our framework):} We may use a \textbf{reduced feature} $y_t$ per experiment (e.g.\ a scalar such as max ROCOF): $\Map(\theta,\xi)=\max_{i,k}|\mathrm{ROCOF}_i[k]|$, $y_t=\Map(\theta,\xi_t)+\epsilon_t$. The posterior update produces $p_t(\theta)=p(\theta\mid\xi_{1:t},y_{1:t})$. A formulation using full $\omega(t)$, $P_e(t)$ would be closer to the paper but heavier computationally.

% -----------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Likelihood (our sBOED extension)}
\label{sec:likelihood}

\textbf{Added by our framework; not from the probing paper.} We assume a Gaussian likelihood for the (possibly scalar) observation $y_t$. At step $t$, we choose $\xi_t$ and observe $y_t$. For any candidate $\theta$, the forward model predicts $\mu(\theta,\xi_t):=\Map(\theta,\xi_t)$. We model $y_t=\mu(\theta,\xi_t)+\epsilon_t$, $\epsilon_t\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma_{\mathrm{feat}}^2)$. So $p(y_t\mid\theta,\xi_t)$ is Gaussian. On the discrete support $\{\theta_{\ninner}\}_{\ninner=1}^{\Ninner}$, $\mu_{\ninner}^{(t)}:=\Map(\theta_{\ninner},\xi_t)$ and
\[
\mathcal{L}_{\ninner}^{(t)}
:=p(y_t\mid\theta_{\ninner},\xi_t)
=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\sigma_{\mathrm{feat}}^2}}
\exp\left(-\frac{\big(y_t-\mu_{\ninner}^{(t)}\big)^2}{2\sigma_{\mathrm{feat}}^2}\right).
\]
Thus $\mathcal{L}_{\ninner}^{(t)}$ is large when the prediction under $\theta_{\ninner}$ is close to $y_t$.

% -----------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Posterior update (our sBOED extension)}
\label{sec:posterior}

\textbf{Added by our framework; not from the probing paper.} The posterior update takes the prior belief over $\theta=(H,K)$, the likelihood of the new observation, and produces the posterior belief. The steps are separated below for clarity.

\paragraph{(a) Prior and posterior over $\theta=(H,K)$.}
The \textbf{prior} at step $t$ is the belief over the latent parameter given past designs and observations: weights $p_{t-1}^{\ninner}=\Pr(\theta=\theta_{\ninner}\mid\xi_{1:t-1},y_{1:t-1})$ on the discrete support $\{\theta_{\ninner}\}_{\ninner=1}^{\Ninner}$. The \textbf{posterior} after observing $y_t$ under design $\xi_t$ is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:posterior}
p_t(\theta)\;=\;p(\theta\mid\xi_{1:t},y_{1:t});\qquad\text{on the support,}\quad p_t^{\ninner}\;=\;\Pr(\theta=\theta_{\ninner}\mid\xi_{1:t},y_{1:t}),\quad \ninner=1,\dots,\Ninner.
\end{equation}
Mathematically: the posterior is the conditional distribution of $\theta$ given all experiments and observations so far.

\paragraph{(b) Likelihood $p(y_t\mid\theta,\xi_t)$.}
The observation model gives the probability of observing $y_t$ when the true parameter is $\theta$ and the design is $\xi_t$. For the Gaussian observation model in Section~\ref{sec:likelihood},
\begin{equation}
p(y_t\mid\theta_{\ninner},\xi_t)\;=\;\mathcal{L}_{\ninner}^{(t)}\;=\;\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\sigma_{\mathrm{feat}}^2}}\,\exp\left(-\frac{\big(y_t-\mu_{\ninner}^{(t)}\big)^2}{2\sigma_{\mathrm{feat}}^2}\right),\qquad \mu_{\ninner}^{(t)}=\Map(\theta_{\ninner},\xi_t).
\end{equation}
So the likelihood is the probability density of $y_t$ under the forward model at $\theta_{\ninner}$.

\paragraph{(c) Discrete weight update (Bayes' rule).}
Bayes' rule states: posterior $\propto$ prior $\times$ likelihood. On the discrete support, the \textbf{unnormalized} posterior weight at $\theta_{\ninner}$ is
\begin{equation}
\tilde p_t^{\ninner}\;=\;p_{t-1}^{\ninner}\,\mathcal{L}_{\ninner}^{(t)}\;=\;p_{t-1}^{\ninner}\,p(y_t\mid\theta_{\ninner},\xi_t),\qquad \ninner=1,\dots,\Ninner.
\end{equation}
Interpretation: support points that predict $y_t$ well (large $\mathcal{L}_{\ninner}^{(t)}$) receive a larger unnormalized weight.

\paragraph{(d) Normalization.}
The unnormalized weights sum to $Z_t$, the marginal likelihood of $y_t$ given $\xi_t$ and the prior. The \textbf{normalization constant} is
\begin{equation}
Z_t\;=\;\sum_{\ninner=1}^{\Ninner}\tilde p_t^{\ninner}.
\end{equation}
The \textbf{normalized posterior weights} are
\begin{equation}
p_t^{\ninner}\;=\;\frac{\tilde p_t^{\ninner}}{Z_t},\qquad \ninner=1,\dots,\Ninner;\qquad\text{hence}\quad \sum_{\ninner=1}^{\Ninner}p_t^{\ninner}=1.
\end{equation}
So $p_t^{\ninner}$ defines a valid discrete distribution over $\theta$.

\paragraph{(e) Log-sum-exp implementation.}
When likelihoods are very small, direct multiplication causes numerical underflow. The update is therefore implemented in log space. Define the log unnormalized weight
\begin{equation}
a_t^{\ninner}\;=\;\log \tilde p_t^{\ninner}\;=\;\log p_{t-1}^{\ninner}-\frac{1}{2}\log(2\pi\sigma_{\mathrm{feat}}^2)-\frac{\big(y_t-\mu_{\ninner}^{(t)}\big)^2}{2\sigma_{\mathrm{feat}}^2}.
\end{equation}
Let $c_t=\max_{\ninner} a_t^{\ninner}$. Then
\begin{equation}
\log Z_t\;=\;c_t+\log\sum_{\ninner'=1}^{\Ninner}\exp\big(a_t^{\ninner'}-c_t\big),
\end{equation}
and the normalized weights are
\begin{equation}
p_t^{\ninner}\;=\;\exp\big(a_t^{\ninner}-\log Z_t\big),\qquad \ninner=1,\dots,\Ninner.
\end{equation}
This log-sum-exp procedure avoids underflow and is numerically stable (Chopin \& Papaspiliopoulos, 2020).

\subsection{Supplementary control, decision quantity, and sequential design objective}
\label{sec:decision}

\textbf{Added by our framework.}
Two separate roles are important: the \textbf{learning model} (probing and posterior update) and the \textbf{evaluation model} (downstream safety and $\gamma^\star(\theta)$). The latent plant parameter is $\theta=(H,K)$ (inertia constant $H$, aggregate droop $K$); $M=2HS_n/\omega_s$ is the derived inertia coefficient used in the dynamics when convenient. Three quantities are kept distinct: \textbf{(a)} probe design $\xi$ is for \emph{learning}---it determines $u_{\mathrm{probe}}(\cdot;\xi)$, which is used only to generate informative observations $y_t$ for posterior updating via $p(y_t\mid\theta,\xi_t)$; \textbf{(b)} $\theta=(H,K)$ is the \emph{latent} parameter we infer; \textbf{(c)} supplementary gain $\gamma$ is the \emph{downstream control decision variable} we choose after learning to satisfy frequency security under the fixed evaluation disturbance $d_{\mathrm{ref}}$. The decision quantity $\gamma^\star(\theta)$ depends only on the evaluation problem (dynamics under $d_{\mathrm{ref}}$), \emph{not} on the probing input.

\subsubsection*{Learning model (probing and posterior).}
The total injection during a probing experiment can be written as $u_i(t)=u_{\mathrm{probe},i}(t;\xi)+u_{\mathrm{ctrl},i}(t;\gamma)$. The probing input $u_{\mathrm{probe}}(\cdot;\xi)$ is chosen by the sBOED policy to obtain observations; the likelihood is $p(y_t\mid\theta,\xi_t)$. The supplementary control $u_{\mathrm{ctrl}}$ is a modeling choice for the physical system but is \emph{not} part of the definition of $\gamma^\star(\theta)$: $\gamma^\star(\theta)$ is defined from the evaluation scenario only (see below). Its functional form is fixed when simulating trajectories; we use the instantaneous nodewise law in \eqref{eq:uctrl_nodewise} (see Section~\ref{sec:taher_motivation}).

\paragraph{Probe input (for learning).}
Following the probing paper, $\xi=(b,A,T_p)$ (bus, amplitude, duration) and
$u_{\mathrm{probe},i}(t;\xi)=A\,h(t;T_p)$ if $i=b$, else $0$, with e.g.\ Hann $h(t;T_p)=\sin^2(\pi t/T_p)$ on $[0,T_p]$. Once $\xi$ is specified, $u_{\mathrm{probe}}(\cdot;\xi)$ is known; it is used only to excite transients for observation $y_t$ and posterior update.

\subsubsection*{Evaluation model (definition of $\gamma^\star(\theta)$).}
The \textbf{evaluation model} is used solely to define the downstream decision quantity $\gamma^\star(\theta)$. It uses the \textbf{fixed} evaluation disturbance $d_{\mathrm{ref}}$ (contingency) and \textbf{does not} include any probing input.

\paragraph{Relation to Taher et al.\ (2019) and our control law.}
\label{sec:taher_motivation}
Taher, Olmi, and Schöll (2019) study synchronization and stability in a power-grid model using \textbf{time-delayed} feedback applied to \textbf{selected important nodes}. Their formulation introduces \textbf{nodewise gain factors} on controlled buses and compares delayed feedback with an \textbf{instantaneous} feedback variant, showing that gain-weighted feedback can improve stability. Our work is inspired by this \textbf{gain-weighted, damping-like} supplementary control idea, but we do \textbf{not} model explicit time delay: a delayed law would couple $u_{\mathrm{ctrl}}(t)$ to past $\omega$ and would complicate the definition of $\gamma^\star(\theta)$ and the MOCU layer. We instead adopt the simpler \textbf{instantaneous} supplementary feedback
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:uctrl_nodewise}
u_{\mathrm{ctrl},i}(t;\gamma,\mathbf g)=-\gamma\, g_i\,\Delta\omega_i(t),
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf g=(g_i)_{i=1}^{N_{\mathrm{bus}}}$ is a fixed nonnegative weight vector (e.g.\ from generator participation in the swing model). When a single scalar gain multiplies aggregate frequency deviation in a reduced model, this collapses to $u_{\mathrm{ctrl}}(t;\gamma)\propto -\gamma\,\Delta\omega(t)$ as used in the learning-model shorthand above. The decision quantity $\gamma^\star(\theta)$ below is defined from the \textbf{evaluation} dynamics and frequency-security constraints under \eqref{eq:uctrl_nodewise} (equivalently \eqref{eq:eval_loop_agg}--\eqref{eq:eval_loop_bus}); it is \textbf{not} taken from Taher et al.

\paragraph{Evaluation dynamics.}
In the evaluation problem, $\theta=(H,K)$ is the latent parameter, $d_{\mathrm{ref}}$ is fixed and known (contingency), and $\gamma$ is the downstream control decision variable. With $M=2HS_n/\omega_s$, the aggregate evaluation dynamics are
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:eval_loop_agg}
M\,\frac{d\Delta\omega}{dt}
=
-\Delta P_e(t;d_{\mathrm{ref}})
-
\left(\frac{K}{2\pi}+D+\gamma\right)\Delta\omega(t).
\end{equation}
The bus-wise evaluation dynamics are
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:eval_loop_bus}
M_i \dot{\Delta\omega}_i(t)=-\Delta P_{e,i}(t;d_{\mathrm{ref}})-\left(\frac{K_i}{2\pi}+D_i+\gamma\right)\Delta\omega_i(t).
\end{equation}
The term linear in $\Delta\omega_i$ in \eqref{eq:eval_loop_bus} is the specialization of \eqref{eq:uctrl_nodewise} when the supplementary channel is modeled as a uniform scalar $\gamma$ on each bus (weights $g_i$ absorbed or set to unity in the reduced notation). For given $\theta$ and $\gamma\ge 0$, the solution $\Delta\omega(t;\theta,\gamma)$ on $[0,T_c]$ is unique. Frequency and ROCOF on the evaluation window are
\begin{equation}
f(t;\theta,\gamma)\;=\;f_0+\frac{\Delta\omega(t;\theta,\gamma)}{2\pi},\qquad \dot f(t;\theta,\gamma)\;=\;\frac{1}{2\pi}\,\frac{d\Delta\omega}{dt}.
\end{equation}
They are defined by the trajectory; no closed-form in $H,K,r_{\max},f_{\min}$ is assumed. $\gamma^\star(\theta)$ is located numerically from this trajectory (see below).

\paragraph{Frequency-security conditions and safe $\gamma$ range.}
On the evaluation window $[0,T_c]$ the two constraints are
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:safety_conditions}
\big|\dot f(t;\theta,\gamma)\big|\le r_{\max},\quad f(t;\theta,\gamma)\ge f_{\min},\qquad \forall t\in[0,T_c].
\end{equation}
(For multi-bus, per bus.) $\gamma$ must be large enough to satisfy the ROCOF condition and large enough to satisfy the minimum-frequency condition. So the admissible safe range for $\gamma$ is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:gamma_range}
\gamma\;\in\;\big[\gamma^\star(\theta),\,\infty\big),
\end{equation}
where $\gamma^\star(\theta)$ is the minimum gain required for frequency security. Define $\gammaROCOF(\theta)=\min\{\gamma\ge 0:\;\sup_{t\in[0,T_c]}|\dot f(t;\theta,\gamma)|\le r_{\max}\}$ and $\gammafreq(\theta)=\min\{\gamma\ge 0:\;\inf_{t\in[0,T_c]}f(t;\theta,\gamma)\ge f_{\min}\}$. Under the monotonicity assumption used by the bisection algorithm (Algorithm~\ref{alg:gamma_star})---each condition alone gives an upper interval of the form $[\bar\gamma,\infty)$---we have
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:gamma_star}
\gamma^\star(\theta)\;=\;\max\big\{\gammaROCOF(\theta),\gammafreq(\theta)\big\},\qquad\text{hence}\quad \gamma\;\in\;\big[\gamma^\star(\theta),\,\infty\big).
\end{equation}
Any $\gamma\ge\gamma^\star(\theta)$ is safe; $\gamma<\gamma^\star(\theta)$ violates at least one constraint. The security constraints are on the evaluation trajectory; $\Delta\omega(t;\theta,\gamma)$ depends on $\gamma$ through the swing equation, so $\gamma^\star(\theta)$ has no simple closed form in $M,K,r_{\max},f_{\min}$ and is located numerically. $\gamma^\star(\theta)$ does not depend on $\xi$ or $u_{\mathrm{probe}}$.

\paragraph{Numerical location of $\gamma^\star(\theta)$.}
In practice, $\gamma^\star(\theta)$ is found by: (1)~choose a candidate $\gamma$; (2)~simulate the evaluation trajectory on $[0,T_c]$ under $d_{\mathrm{ref}}$; (3)~check both frequency-security constraints; (4)~bracket safe vs.\ unsafe $\gamma$; (5)~bisect under the monotonicity / upper-interval assumption. This is Algorithm~\ref{alg:gamma_star}.

\paragraph{Set notation (optional).}
$\GammaROCOF(\theta)=\{\gamma\ge 0:\;\sup_t|\dot f(t;\theta,\gamma)|\le r_{\max}\}$, $\Gammafreq(\theta)=\{\gamma\ge 0:\;\inf_t f(t;\theta,\gamma)\ge f_{\min}\}$; under monotonicity they are upper intervals. $\Gammasafe(\theta)=\GammaROCOF(\theta)\cap\Gammafreq(\theta)$ and $\gamma^\star(\theta)=\inf\Gammasafe(\theta)$.

\paragraph{Connection to MOCU.}
The posterior $p_t(\theta)$ expresses uncertainty in $\theta=(H,K)$. Each $\theta$ implies a minimum safe gain $\gamma^\star(\theta)$, so uncertainty in $\theta$ induces uncertainty in $\gamma^\star(\theta)$. MOCU measures this downstream decision uncertainty: the posterior expected decision error in $\gamma^\star(\theta)$ when we commit to a point estimate $\hat\gamma_t$. The sequential design objective is to choose probes that reduce uncertainty in the control threshold $\gamma^\star(\theta)$, not only parameter uncertainty in the abstract. Dependence chain:
\[
\xi_t\;\longrightarrow\;y_t\;\longrightarrow\;p_t(\theta)\;\longrightarrow\;\text{induced uncertainty in }\gamma^\star(\theta)\;\longrightarrow\;\MOCU(p_t).
\]
Under absolute-error loss, the Bayes-optimal point estimator is any weighted median: $\hat\gamma_t\in\wmed(\{\gamma^\star(\theta_{\ninner})\},\{p_t^{\ninner}\})$. The \textbf{mean objective cost of uncertainty (MOCU)} is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:mocu_gamma}
\MOCU(p_t)\;=\;\mathbb{E}_{\theta\sim p_t}\big[|\gamma^\star(\theta)-\hat\gamma_t|\big]\;=\;\sum_{\ninner=1}^{\Ninner}p_t^{\ninner}\,\big|\gamma^\star(\theta_{\ninner})-\hat\gamma_t\big|.
\end{equation}
So the goal is to reduce uncertainty in the minimum safe gain $\gamma^\star(\theta)$, not merely in $\theta$ itself.

\paragraph{Sequential design objective.}
We use a single indexing convention: at step $t$ we have belief $p_{t-1}$; we choose design $\xi_t\in\Xi$; we observe $y_t$ and update to $p_t$. The sequential design policy selects $\xi_t$ to minimize the expected MOCU of the resulting posterior:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:sboed_gamma}
\xi_t^\star
=
\argmin_{\xi\in\Xi}
\mathbb{E}_{y_t\mid p_{t-1},\xi}
\big[
\MOCU(p_t)
\big].
\end{equation}
The expectation is over $y_t$ given the current belief $p_{t-1}$ and the chosen design $\xi$; $p_t$ is the posterior after observing that $y_t$. The dependence chain is
\[
\text{probe design }\xi_t
\;\longrightarrow\;
\text{observation }y_t
\;\longrightarrow\;
\text{posterior }p_t(\theta)
\;\longrightarrow\;
\text{induced uncertainty in }\gamma^\star(\theta)
\;\longrightarrow\;
\MOCU(p_t).
\]
Probing is used for information acquisition; the decision objective is the minimum supplementary control effort required to satisfy the frequency-security conditions under $d_{\mathrm{ref}}$.

\section{Sequential design and learning}
\label{sec:sequential}

This section gives \textbf{Algorithm~\ref{alg:dad_mocu_loop}}: the
\emph{online} execution of one episode of sequential design. The goal is
to choose the probing design $\xi_t$ at each step so that, in expectation, the mean objective cost of uncertainty in the downstream decision quantity $\gamma^\star(\theta)$ is reduced. This cost is $\MOCU(p_t)$, defined in \eqref{eq:mocu_gamma}. At each step, we update the belief from $p_{t-1}$ to $p_t$, compute the Bayes estimator $\hat\gamma_t$, and evaluate $\MOCU(p_t)$.

The algorithm assumes a \textbf{pre-trained} amortized policy
\(\pi_\phi\) that maps the experiment history
\(h_{t-1}=\{(\xi_s,y_s)\}_{s=1}^{t-1}\) to a distribution over the next
probing design \(\xi_t\). In one online episode, we only execute the
policy and update the posterior with the single realized observation at
each step.

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{Sequential Bayesian Experimental Design (sBOED) using Amortized Policy}
\label{alg:dad_mocu_loop}
\small
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require Network \(\mathcal{G}\); discrete support \(\{\theta_{\ninner}\}_{\ninner=1}^{\Ninner}\) and prior \(\{p_0^{\ninner}\}_{\ninner=1}^{\Ninner}\); precomputed \(\gamma^\star_{\ninner}=\gamma^\star(\theta_{\ninner})\) for all \(\ninner\) (from closed-loop evaluation under \(d_{\mathrm{ref}}\)); noise \(\sigma_{\mathrm{feat}}\); horizon \(T\); \textbf{pre-trained} amortized policy \(\pi_\phi\).
\Ensure One episode: beliefs \(\{p_t\}_{t=0}^{T}\), posterior Bayes estimators \(\{\hat\gamma_t\}_{t=0}^{T}\), and posterior uncertainties \(\{\MOCU(p_t)\}_{t=0}^{T}\).

\State \(h_0 \gets \emptyset\)
\State Compute \(\hat\gamma_0 \in \wmed\big(\{\gamma^\star_{\ninner}\}_{\ninner=1}^{\Ninner},\{p_0^{\ninner}\}_{\ninner=1}^{\Ninner}\big)\)
\State \(\MOCU(p_0) \gets \sum_{\ninner=1}^{\Ninner} p_0^{\ninner}\,\big|\gamma^\star_{\ninner}-\hat\gamma_0\big|\)

\For{\(t=1,\dots,T\)}
    \State \Comment{\textbf{1. Design phase}}
    \State \(\xi_t \sim \pi_\phi(\cdot \mid h_{t-1})\)

    \State \Comment{\textbf{2. Physical observation}}
    \State \(y_t \gets \Map(\theta_{\mathrm{true}}, \xi_t) + \epsilon_t\), where \(\epsilon_t \sim \mathcal{N}(0,\sigma_{\mathrm{feat}}^2)\)

    \State \Comment{\textbf{3. Exact discrete belief update (log-domain)}}
    \For{\(\ninner=1,\dots,\Ninner\)}
        \State \(\log \tilde{p}_t^{\ninner} \gets \log p_{t-1}^{\ninner}
        - \frac{1}{2}\log(2\pi\sigma_{\mathrm{feat}}^2)
        - \frac{\big(y_t-\Map(\theta_{\ninner},\xi_t)\big)^2}{2\sigma_{\mathrm{feat}}^2}\)
    \EndFor
    \State \(a_t \gets \max_{\ninner}\log \tilde{p}_t^{\ninner}\)
    \State \(\log Z_t \gets a_t + \log\sum_{\ninner'=1}^{\Ninner}\exp\big(\log \tilde{p}_t^{\ninner'}-a_t\big)\)
    \For{\(\ninner=1,\dots,\Ninner\)}
        \State \(p_t^{\ninner} \gets \exp\big(\log \tilde{p}_t^{\ninner}-\log Z_t\big)\)
    \EndFor

    \State \Comment{\textbf{4. Posterior decision summary}}
    \State Compute \(\hat\gamma_t \in \wmed\big(\{\gamma^\star_{\ninner}\}_{\ninner=1}^{\Ninner},\{p_t^{\ninner}\}_{\ninner=1}^{\Ninner}\big)\)
    \State \(\MOCU(p_t) \gets \sum_{\ninner=1}^{\Ninner} p_t^{\ninner}\,\big|\gamma^\star_{\ninner}-\hat\gamma_t\big|\)

    \State \Comment{\textbf{5. History update}}
    \State \(h_t \gets h_{t-1} \cup \{(\xi_t,y_t)\}\)
\EndFor

\State \Return \(\{p_t\}_{t=0}^{T}\), \(\{\hat\gamma_t\}_{t=0}^{T}\), \(\{\MOCU(p_t)\}_{t=0}^{T}\)
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\section{Computing the model-specific minimum safe gain}
\label{sec:gamma_star}

This section gives \textbf{Algorithm~\ref{alg:gamma_star}} for
computing the downstream decision quantity
\(\gamma^\star(\theta)\) defined in \eqref{eq:gamma_star}. For a given
plant model \(\theta=(H,K)\) (with \(M=2HS_n/\omega_s\) in the dynamics), the goal is to find the smallest
additional feedback gain \(\gamma\ge 0\) such that the \emph{evaluation} closed-loop
trajectory (dynamics \eqref{eq:eval_loop_agg} or \eqref{eq:eval_loop_bus} under \(d_{\mathrm{ref}}\), no probing input) satisfies \eqref{eq:safety_conditions} on \([0,T_c]\).

Define the safety predicate
\[
\Safe(\gamma;\theta)=\text{True}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\text{the solution of the evaluation dynamics \eqref{eq:eval_loop_agg} (or \eqref{eq:eval_loop_bus}) under }(\theta,\gamma,d_{\mathrm{ref}})
\text{ satisfies \eqref{eq:safety_conditions} on }[0,T_c].
\]
Then
\[
\gamma^\star(\theta)
=
\inf\{\gamma\ge 0:\Safe(\gamma;\theta)=\text{True}\}.
\]
Under the monotonicity assumption that \(\Gammasafe(\theta)\) is of the form
\([\gamma^\star(\theta),\infty)\), \(\gamma^\star(\theta)\) can be
computed by bracketing followed by bisection.

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{Compute the model-specific minimum safe gain \(\gamma^\star(\theta)\)}
\label{alg:gamma_star}
\small
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require Parameter \(\theta\); network \(\mathcal{G}\); limits \((r_{\max},f_{\min})\); horizon \(T_c\); fixed evaluation scenario \(d_{\mathrm{ref}}\); tolerance \(\varepsilon\); initial upper bound \(\gamma_{\max}\); hard cap \(\gamma_{\mathrm{cap}}\); maximum expansion steps \(N_{\mathrm{expand}}\).
\Ensure Approximation of \(\gamma^\star(\theta)\); return \(\gamma_{\mathrm{cap}}\) if no safe gain is found up to the cap.

\State \textbf{Define} \(\Safe(\gamma;\theta)=\text{True}\) iff the trajectory of the \emph{evaluation} dynamics \eqref{eq:eval_loop_agg} (or \eqref{eq:eval_loop_bus}) under \((\theta,\gamma,d_{\mathrm{ref}})\) satisfies \eqref{eq:safety_conditions} on \([0,T_c]\).

\State \Comment{\textbf{1. Bracketing phase}}
\State \(\gamma_L \gets 0\)
\State \(\gamma_U \gets \gamma_{\max}\)

\For{\(k=1,\dots,N_{\mathrm{expand}}\)}
    \If{\(\Safe(\gamma_U;\theta)\) is True}
        \State \textbf{break}
    \EndIf
    \State \(\gamma_U \gets \min(2\gamma_U,\gamma_{\mathrm{cap}})\)
    \If{\(\gamma_U=\gamma_{\mathrm{cap}}\)}
        \State \textbf{break}
    \EndIf
\EndFor

\If{\textbf{not} \(\Safe(\gamma_U;\theta)\)}
    \State \Return \(\gamma_{\mathrm{cap}}\)
\EndIf

\State \Comment{\textbf{2. Bisection phase}}
\While{\(\gamma_U-\gamma_L>\varepsilon\)}
    \State \(\gamma_M \gets (\gamma_L+\gamma_U)/2\)
    \If{\(\Safe(\gamma_M;\theta)\) is True}
        \State \(\gamma_U \gets \gamma_M\)
    \Else
        \State \(\gamma_L \gets \gamma_M\)
    \EndIf
\EndWhile

\State \Return \(\gamma_U\)
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\section{Limitations and modeling choices}
\label{sec:limitations}

This section states limitations and modeling choices explicitly so that the scope of the framework is clear; each subsection indicates what is assumed and possible extensions.

\subsection{Sequential design and myopic heuristic}

The goal is to reduce the terminal cost of uncertainty in the decision quantity $\gamma^\star(\theta)$, for example by minimizing $\mathbb{E}[\MOCU(p_T)]$. The sequential design is order-dependent and $\MOCU(p_t)$ is typically non-increasing in $t$. A myopic rule that at each step $t$ chooses $\xi_t$ to minimize $\mathbb{E}_{y_t\mid p_{t-1},\xi_t}[\MOCU(p_t)]$ is tractable but generally suboptimal for terminal $\MOCU(p_T)$.

\subsection{Parametrization and tractability}

The latent parameter $\theta=(H,K)$ (or multi-bus extensions) may be discretized on a grid or represented by particles. Implementations may use reduced parametrizations or Sequential Monte Carlo (Chopin \& Papaspiliopoulos, 2020; Drovandi et al., 2013); the algorithms apply to any such belief representation.

\subsection{Observation model and noise}

In our simplified implementation, each experiment yields a scalar feature $y_t=\Map(\theta,\xi_t)+\epsilon_t$ with Gaussian noise; the probing paper instead works with full $\omega(t)$ and $P_e(t)$ and an integrated relation to estimate $(H,K)$. Using richer observations would be closer to the probing paper but more computationally demanding for sBOED.

\subsection{Decision quantity: task-oriented vs.\ estimation-oriented OED}

The probing paper (Peng et al., 2024) is estimation-oriented: it chooses probes to estimate $(H,K)$ accurately. Here the decision quantity is the downstream control requirement $\gamma^\star(\theta)$, and the sBOED rule chooses $\xi_t$ to reduce the mean objective cost of uncertainty $\MOCU(p_t)$ in $\gamma^\star(\theta)$, which is a task-oriented objective rather than generic parameter-variance reduction.

\subsection{Bisection validity (optional $\gamma^\star$ computation)}

Algorithm~\ref{alg:gamma_star} computes $\gamma^\star(\theta)$ by bracketing and bisection, assuming the safety predicate is non-decreasing in $\gamma$ over the operational range. If this monotonicity fails, one would need a more general search for $\gamma^\star(\theta)$; this affects only the numerical computation of $\gamma^\star(\theta)$, not the definition of $\MOCU(p_t)$ or the sBOED formulation.
